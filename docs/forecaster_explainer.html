0. The four names

Fine-tuned encoder train

TorNet’s backbone only, copy 1. Weights start from the published model and move in training. Shared by both heads.

Frozen detector frozen

The complete published TorNet, copy 2, backbone plus max-pool head. Weights never move. Scores the floor, the ceiling and the forecast; in some configs it also sits inside the loss as a critic.

Direct route head

The classification head’s own logit for +5 and +10 min. No frozen detector involved.

Indirect route judge

The drawn forecast chip, read by the frozen detector. Its score tells whether the drawing carries the tornado signature.

3. Which samples count as prediction

Every sample has four scans about five minutes apart, each with its own tornado label. Choose how many scans the model sees; the rest are what it predicts. A tornadic sample is a pre-onset case only when no input scan has the tornado and the last scan does.

4. What the runs measured

Two short probe runs on a capped fold B (6,000 training files, 4 epochs), then the three full fold B runs (2013 + 2015 training, 6 epochs, overnight on the laptop, 2026-10-14). All scored on the 2014 test year: 2,546 samples, 220 pre-onset tornado cases. Pre-onset cases against warned storms, AUC. The frozen detector reads three chips; the trained head gives its own answer.

Runs live in results/forecast_fold_b/.

What fold B settled. The drawn forecast reaches the persistence floor and stops there, with four times the probe's data and six epochs: the indirect route adds nothing over copying the last scan, in pixels or through the judge. The trained head is the only output above the floor, about +0.02 WC AUC at +10 min and at the edge of significance; at +5 min nothing beats the floor. The checkpoint rule (best validation head AUC) picked epoch 1 for the 12ch run, so its drawing is under-trained; rescoring the last checkpoint and a route-aware selection rule come before any more loss work.
